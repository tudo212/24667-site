First robot: one flywheel, adjustable arc

451 × 422 × 346 mm, 10.8 kg, 2.3 m/s. Our first season together, all the way to the China Finals.

01 / Design overview

Intake, transfer, launch, lift

01 Intake

Roller and rubber bands

A 2000 rpm roller uses the bands' centrifugal force to pull artifacts in, 0.3 s each.

02 Transfer

Three stages

Asymmetric printed guides stop two artifacts jamming the entrance at once.

03 Launch

One flywheel, adjustable

A servo drives a timing belt to change the launch angle between near and far.

04 Lift

Tool-free lift

Built for the base task in the last fifteen seconds; comes apart without tools.

A 90° gearbox to free up storage

Five carbon plates and four goBILDA mecanum wheels. The motors do not sit on the wheels; a custom gearbox turns them 90°, leaving the middle of the robot free for artifacts.

One PinPoint gyro and two dead wheels feed three vectors to RoadRunner. It worked in our first year: twelve artifacts in autonomous.

  • Heading error< 1°
  • Position error< 5 mm
  • Autonomous12 artifacts

One flywheel, distance by angle

The first build had a fixed angle and could only shoot close. We added a servo and a timing belt to push a printed bracket out, so the arc is adjustable between near and far.

The flywheel went from soft to hard, with a 30-degree silicone ring and six stainless plates to hold speed during a burst. Exit speed about 12 m/s.

03 / Iteration

Two versions in one year

V1.0

Make it work

Fixed angle, near shots only; the soft flywheel lost too much speed in a burst. The first build connected intake, transfer, launch and lift.

V2.0

Make it reach

Hard flywheel with silicone ring and six weights; adjustable arc; Limelight auto-aim; wider intake mouth for a bigger margin.

04 / Code

Three small things

Linear PID

When the battery sags, a fixed-power shot lands short. We measured five power levels at different voltages and found speed close to linear in power, so we trim by the speed error instead: P = 0.0001, converging in about twenty steps.

One-button return

Teleop means going back to the shooting zone again and again. After the first shot we store the pose and return to it from anywhere with one button.

A light sensor in the launch channel stops Transmit220 once the first artifact is seated, so it cannot push the ball back out.

06 / Build log

Starting with a screwdriver

  • 2025-10-28Kuang Boyu

    Drive practice: starting and switching OpModes, using the driver station. Four of us ran two rounds each and scored 80, 89, 90 and 87. Judging distance and hand-off between drivers were the weak points.

  • 2025-10-27Huang Linxin

    Walked through the autonomous options: too little power and the artifact falls short, too much and it spins out; sometimes it is intaken but never reaches the launcher. Teleop controls explained.

  • 2025-10-20Zhong Yuexin

    Swapped acrylic for carbon fibre. Discussed artifact colour strategy, which we could not detect yet. The lift became tool-free so the robot can change state quickly in a match.

  • 2025-10-19Zhong Yuexin

    Learned the drill and tapping. Got the outline of the Java codebase. Argued about mounting plates to the lift tube and settled on a glued hollow cylinder expanded by a screw.

  • 2025-10-15Yi Quanmin

    Coach Wu demonstrated the robot and its weak points: accuracy and arc at the far zone, collector speed, no way to reorder artifacts, battery capacity and chassis vibration.
